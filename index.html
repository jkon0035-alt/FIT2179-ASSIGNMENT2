Chapter 01 · Tobacco

The Last Generation of Smokers?

Australia's plain-packaging laws and excise taxes are among the world's toughest. Since 2001, daily smoking has nearly halved — from 19.4% to 8.3%. But who is still smoking, and where? The data reveals a habit increasingly concentrated in older age groups, regional areas, and certain states.

Chapter 02 · E-Cigarettes

The Vaping Surge — A Crisis Among the Young

While smoking fell, vaping exploded. Between 2016 and 2023 the proportion of Australians who currently vape tripled. The rise is sharpest among 18–24 year olds, raising serious concerns that a new generation is becoming addicted to nicotine through a different device.

Key insight: Vaping among 18–24 year olds jumped from 2.8% in 2016 to 20.6% in 2023 — a sevenfold increase in just seven years.

Chapter 03 · Illicit Drugs

Cannabis Dominates, Cocaine Surges

Around 1 in 6 Australians used an illicit drug in the past year. Cannabis remains the most widely used substance, but cocaine use has nearly quadrupled since 2001. Drug use peaks sharply in the 20s and varies dramatically by substance — and the hospitalisations tell a particularly stark story about alcohol.

Chapter 04 · Geography

Where You Live Shapes Your Substance Risk

Substance use is not evenly spread across Australia. Remote communities record smoking rates nearly three times higher than major cities. Drug use also rises with remoteness. Interestingly, vaping runs the opposite pattern — higher in cities. This map tells a public health equity story: two different crises unfolding in two different Australias.

Chart 07 · Symbol Map + Radio Buttons + Legend Filter

How Remoteness Shapes Substance Use Across Australia

Click a radio button to switch between smoking, vaping and drug use rates. Click a legend item to highlight that remoteness category. Bubble size = rate. Hover for all three values at once.

💡

Key insight: Remote/Very Remote Australians smoke at 20.4% — nearly 3× the 7.0% in Major Cities. For vaping it reverses: cities vape more (3.9%) than remote areas (3.7%). Two very different crises, two different geographies.

Source: AIHW NDSHS 2022–23, Table S3.5 — Smoking and drug use by remoteness area
